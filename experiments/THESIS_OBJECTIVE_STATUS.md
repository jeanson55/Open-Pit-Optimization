# Thesis objective progress and remaining work

This status maps work in this project to the five formal objectives in `MSC TOPIC (2).docx`. The evidence includes the completed Experiment E, adaptive solver and boundary-refinement experiments, the four-deposit partition grid, and grouped V3.6 validation. Objectives are marked complete only where the current evidence covers the stated outcome.

## Objective status

| # | Formal objective | Status | Evidence and remaining gap |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Develop an intelligent graph partitioning algorithm that decomposes block models into computationally efficient subgraphs. | Implemented and evaluated; no superior hybrid established | Spatial, METIS, and spectral methods are paired across four deposits at multiple counts; Experiment E includes full bauxitemed solves. Spatial remained the strongest bauxitemed method: final NPV 28,610,119 versus METIS 25,472,696 and balanced spectral 19,590,009. Hybrid V3.2 selected Spatial. Hybrid V4.1 added graph-driven boundary moves and equal-size swaps, passed 100 directed-graph checks (4,833 move-delta and 4,691 swap-delta comparisons), but made no moves on full bauxitemed: it reproduced Spatial's 18,800 cut arcs, 1.557 max/mean size ratio, 878 initial violations, 3,864 repair additions, zero final violations, and NPV 28,610,119. Its partition runtime was 5.16 s versus 1.58 s Spatial, with no solution benefit. Record this negative finding and do not spend more time on local Hybrid V4 tuning before write-up. |
| 2 | Design an adaptive decision engine that selects LG or HPF for each partition based on graph and geological characteristics. | Partial | `optimization/adaptive_solver.py` supports heuristic and learned local selection with an LG size guard. The full partitioned runs used HPF for every tested large partition. The timing set now has 147 paired samples (130 LG-faster, 17 HPF-faster), including 48 larger-patch pairs on four deposits. On the four 3D deposits alone, the runtime model reaches 84.8% accuracy versus 86.4% for always-LG, and its observed runtime sum (72.02 s) is much slower than always-HPF (26.16 s). A decision tree has 90.4% / 77.1% accuracy / balanced accuracy, but its runtime sum (38.67 s) also loses to always-HPF. Continue collecting HPF-faster cases and improve held-out runtime cost before deployment. |
| 3 | Develop dynamic boundary refinement to ensure global feasibility and preserve slope precedence. | Implemented and evaluated for feasibility | The solver repairs all observed final pits to zero precedence violations. The best-setting boundary runs improved NPV on all four deposits, with 0.028% to 1.215% gains over add-only, and retained zero violations. A new deterministic verification experiment checks 100 random small DAGs: all 100 HPF closure values matched exhaustive enumeration, and all 100 refined pits remained feasible without losing the add-only incumbent. This supports implementation correctness on small cases but does not prove a global optimum or scaling behavior. |
| 4 | Integrate machine learning to predict the most efficient optimization strategy for each partition. | Partial | Solver-runtime and V3.6 partition models exist. The expanded solver model has 147 timing labels but loses to simple baselines on observed runtime; the four-3D-dataset classifier improves balanced accuracy, but the decision tree still takes longer than always-HPF. The V3.6 grid composite gets 7/12 matched pairs versus 8/12 for always-Spatial and has 3.890% mean regret versus 0.198% for that baseline. A new V3.7 joint method/count pilot uses 28 candidate outcomes (Spatial/METIS at k=4,8,12; spectral at k=4). LODO accuracy is 75% and regret 0.102%, exactly matching both a training-selected Spatial count and fixed Spatial k=4. It selects Spatial k=4 in every holdout and does not establish selector improvement. Its selected-run mean time omits the cost of generating candidate features; do not deploy. More independent geology and measurable held-out benefit are needed. |
| 5 | Evaluate the framework against standalone LG and HPF implementations on industrial-scale datasets. | Completed on two full industrial models | Full LiteralLG/HPF benchmarks completed on bauxitemed (240,502 nodes) and cucase (1,045,952 nodes). Both solvers returned exact membership agreement (Jaccard 1.0), equal NPV, and zero precedence violations on both models. Bauxitemed NPV was 29,690,715; optimized-run times were LiteralLG 13,206.654 s and HPF 23.391 s (564.6x). Cucase NPV was 19,444,156; times were LiteralLG 5,939.303 s and HPF 105.417 s (56.3x), with whole-process peak RSS 6,266.4 MiB. The partitioned framework has also been compared with global HPF across the four 3D deposits. This fulfills the objective at the demonstrated two-model direct-comparison scope; broader direct LiteralLG coverage on CUPIPE and McLaughlinGeo is optional further validation, not a blocker to the write-up. |

Both visualization builds use existing exact membership and do not rerun optimization.

Objective 1's full bauxitemed Hybrid V4.1 comparison is complete and found no improvement over Spatial; do not tune this local search further before write-up. Objective 5 now has direct LiteralLG/HPF agreement on two full models; do not spend more hours on additional full-scale runs unless the thesis committee explicitly requests broader coverage.
2. Keep the ML results as evaluated negative findings: the learned selector did not beat simple baselines. Do not spend time tuning on the same small set of independent deposits.
3. Audit tables and figures against their raw result files, record environment/version information, and ensure every reported “LG” method is identified as LiteralLG or NetworkX max-flow.
4. Complete thesis results/discussion using `experiments/THESIS_RESULTS_SYNTHESIS.md`, then prepare the final reproducibility appendix and limitations.
5. Retain the current “Partial” objective labels where cross-deposit generalization or extra full-model direct comparisons have not been demonstrated; do not relabel those objectives complete solely to shorten the remaining work.
